The mutation

One swapped letter changes one piece of the protein

DNA is read in triplets of letters (), and each triplet specifies one piece of the protein (an ). In R337H, a G became an A: the triplet CGC became CAC, and piece 337 of p53 swapped arginine for histidine.8

Why is it common in Brazil?

refs.1020

The variant came from Portuguese or Spanish ancestors and was passed down generation after generation in Brazil. That is why it is much more common here, especially in the South and Southeast: it is the .1020

The protein

p53, the cells' “guardian”, works in teams of four

p53 watches over DNA: it orders defects to be repaired, pauses cell division or eliminates the cell if the damage is severe. It acts in groups of four copies, and R337H sits right in the part that joins these copies together, the .78

Research gap

What still needed to be understood, and why study it

The link between R337H and the syndrome was already known. What was missing was bringing together the clinical, population and structural evidence.

Gap

An integrated view

The link was already known, but a view that brought all the evidence together was missing.

Read full text

The research gap of this work lies in the fact that, although the TP53 R337H variant is already recognized as associated with Li-Fraumeni syndrome, there is still a need to understand in an integrated way how its clinical, population and structural features contribute to cancer predisposition.

How common is the variant in the world population?

gnomAD v4.1.1

Answer: very rare worldwide

It gathers the DNA of hundreds of thousands of people. R337H appeared in only 5 of 1.6 million copies of the gene, with no person. But the database has few Brazilians: here it is about a thousand times more common.

Conclusion

R337H causes disease, is rare worldwide and common in Brazil's South and Southeast, makes p53 less stable and appears in several tumors. But, on its own, it does not determine who will get cancer.
